# Startup Funding Sources

A comprehensive guide to capital sources, ranked by relevance to startups.

## Part I - Startup and Company Funding

### 1. Equity Funding

- **Pre-seed / Seed**
  - Angel investors and angel groups: AngelList, Golden Seeds, Pipeline Angels.
  - Pre-seed funds: Hustle Fund, Precursor, Afore.
  - Accelerators: YC, Techstars, 500, MassChallenge, Plug and Play; typically $125K-$500K for equity.
  - Studios: Atomic, Pioneer Square Labs, Betaworks.
- **Venture Capital**
  - Traditional VCs by stage: seed, Series A through late stage.
  - Corporate VCs: GV, Intel Capital, Salesforce Ventures, M12, Comcast Ventures.
  - Strategic investors.
- **Alternative Equity**
  - Crowdfunding equity: Republic, Wefunder, StartEngine; Reg CF up to $5M.
  - Reg A+ with a $75M cap.
  - Family offices and sovereign wealth funds.
- **Family Offices & UHNW Individuals**
  - Single-family offices (SFOs): direct deals from wealthy families.
  - Multi-family offices (MFOs): Bessemer Trust, BBR Partners, and similar groups.
  - Tiger 21 and YPO networks.
  - Angel syndicate platforms: AngelList, Sweater Ventures, Republic.
  - Rolling funds.
  - Search fund model for acquiring rather than starting companies.

### 2. Accelerators, Studios & Founder Programs

- **Top-Tier General Accelerators**
  - Y Combinator: $500K standard deal ($125K for 7% plus $375K on uncapped SAFE), 3-month SF batches twice a year.
  - Techstars: $120K, 3-month programs across many cities and verticals.
  - 500 Global, formerly 500 Startups: $150K, global presence.
  - Alchemist Accelerator: enterprise-focused, about $36K.
  - AngelPad: NYC/SF, small batches, $120K.
  - Entrepreneurs Roundtable Accelerator (ERA): NYC, $100K.
- **Studios & Pre-Accelerators**
  - Pioneer: remote tournament-style, $20K plus path to YC-like terms.
  - On Deck: fellowship model for founders, engineering leaders, and others.
  - South Park Commons: community plus $400K founder fellowship.
  - Antler: pre-team accelerator that helps founders find co-founders, global.
  - EF (Entrepreneur First): pre-team model in London, Bangalore, Singapore, and Berlin.
  - Atomic, Pioneer Square Labs, Betaworks, Expa: venture studios that build companies with founders.
  - HF0 (Hacker Fellowship Zero): SF residency, no equity.
- **Big Tech / Corporate Programs**
  - Google for Startups Accelerator: equity-free, vertical tracks including AI, climate, Black founders, and women founders.
  - Microsoft for Startups Founders Hub: credits and go-to-market support, no equity.
  - AWS Activate: up to $100K in credits.
  - NVIDIA Inception: free, GPU credits and technical support.
  - Meta, Salesforce, Oracle, IBM: startup programs.
  - Stripe Atlas: incorporation and tooling; accelerator-adjacent.
- **Vertical-Specific Accelerators**
  - AI/ML: AI Grant (Nat Friedman and Daniel Gross), $250K plus compute; South Park Commons AI; Conviction Embed.
  - Biotech / Health: IndieBio (SOSV), $525K in SF/NYC for deep tech bio; Y Combinator Bio track; Illumina Accelerator; JLABS (J&J), free lab space and no equity; MassChallenge HealthTech; Cedars-Sinai, Mayo Clinic, and Texas Medical Center accelerators; StartX, Stanford-affiliated and equity-free.
  - Climate / Hard Tech: Activate, formerly Cyclotron Road, a 2-year national lab fellowship with about $100K stipend and no equity; Breakthrough Energy Fellows; Greentown Labs in Boston/Houston; Third Derivative; Elemental Excelerator, $500K+ and Hawaii-based; Urban-X (MINI/Urban Us) for urban tech.
  - Fintech: F-Prime, Plug and Play Fintech, Mastercard Start Path, Visa Accelerator, Barclays Rise.
  - Enterprise / B2B: Alchemist, Acceleprise now Forum Ventures, Heavybit for devtools and infrastructure.
  - Crypto / Web3: a16z Crypto Startup School (CSS), equity-free; Alliance DAO; Orange DAO; Outlier Ventures.
  - Defense / Aerospace / Government: AFWERX, NSIN, Catalyst Accelerator (Space Force), In-Q-Tel (CIA strategic investment), Starburst Aerospace.
  - Robotics / Hardware: HAX (SOSV), Newark/Shenzhen, $250K+; MassRobotics; Bolt.
- **Demographic-Focused Founder Programs**
  - HBCU Founders Initiative, Founder Gym, Backstage Capital programs.
  - All Raise, Female Founders Alliance.
  - Visible Hands for Black, Latinx, and Indigenous founders: $175K.
  - Latinx Startup Alliance, Camelback Ventures.
  - DivInc, Black Ambition (Pharrell Williams).
  - StartOut for LGBTQ+ founders.
  - Bunker Labs, Patriot Boot Camp for veterans.
  - Unshackled Ventures, One Way Ventures, Immigrants Rising for immigrant founders.
  - Gold House, AAPI Founders Fund, Operator Collective for Asian American founders.
  - Native Women Lead, ONABEN, Change Labs, Indigenous Innovation Initiative.
  - Female Founders Fund, BBG Ventures, Pipeline Angels, Golden Seeds, SheEO/Coralus, Halogen Ventures, Springbank Collective, How Women Invest, 37 Angels.
  - Gaingels, an LGBTQ+ investor network, and Lesbians Who Tech.
  - 2Gether-International, Disability:IN, Enable Ventures, Remarkable Accelerator for disabled founders.
  - VetCap, Hivers and Strivers, Task Force Movement, StreetShares Foundation, Warrior Rising for veteran founders.
- **University-Affiliated**
  - StartX (Stanford), no equity.
  - Harvard i-lab, MIT Delta v, Cornell eLab, NYU Endless Frontier Labs.
  - Creative Destruction Lab (Toronto, but global): deep tech, mentor-driven, no equity for participation.
  - Berkeley SkyDeck.
- **International Accelerators**
  - EF, Antler, Seedcamp in Europe.
  - Startupbootcamp, global and vertical-focused.
  - Founders Factory in London.
  - Station F programs in Paris.
  - YC-style programs in Asia: Sequoia Surge, Z Fellows, Iterative.
  - Israel: 8200 EISP, The Junction.
- **Non-Equity Communities**
  - South Park Commons, On Deck, HF0, Founders Inc, AGI House.
  - Indie Hackers: community, not funding.
  - Z Fellows: $10K, 1 week, no equity, founder-quality signal.

### 3. Non-Dilutive Funding (Grants & Awards)

- **Federal Government (US)**
  - SBIR/STTR: Small Business Innovation Research and Small Business Technology Transfer programs across 11 agencies: NIH, NSF, DOE, DoD, NASA, USDA, DOT, ED, EPA, DHS, DOC. About $4B/year. Phase I ($50K-$300K), Phase II ($750K-$2M), Phase III commercialization.
  - NSF beyond SBIR: Convergence Accelerator, Partnerships for Innovation, I-Corps.
  - NIH: direct grants including R01, R21, R43/R44, and institute-specific funding.
  - DOE: ARPA-E, loan guarantees, Office of Technology Transitions.
  - DoD: DIU, AFWERX, SOFWERX, Army xTechSearch, Navy SBIR.
  - NASA: iTech, Tipping Point.
  - USDA: Rural Business Development Grants, REAP.
  - EPA: Green Chemistry and environmental technology grants.
  - NIST: manufacturing extension and advanced manufacturing.
  - DARPA: solicitations for breakthrough technology.
  - IARPA: intelligence community R&D.
  - BARDA: Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority for biodefense and pandemic preparedness.
- **State & Local**
  - State economic development agencies; every state has one.
  - State-specific SBIR matching programs.
  - City-level innovation funds: NYCEDC, LA, Boston, and others.
  - Opportunity Zones programs.
  - State tax credits: R&D, hiring, investment.
  - State Small Business Credit Initiative (SSBCI) programs in every state.
- **International Grants**
  - EU: Horizon Europe, EIC Accelerator up to EUR17.5M, Eureka.
  - UK: Innovate UK, British Business Bank.
  - Canada: IRAP, SR&ED tax credits, Strategic Innovation Fund.
  - Israel: Israel Innovation Authority.
  - Germany: EXIST, ZIM.
  - Singapore: Enterprise Singapore, Startup SG.
  - France: Bpifrance, La French Tech.
  - Australia: R&D Tax Incentive, Accelerating Commercialisation.
  - Japan: J-Startup, NEDO.
- **Industry-Specific Grant Programs**
  - Biotech / Health: BARDA, CARB-X, JDRF T1D Fund, disease foundations.
  - Climate: Breakthrough Energy, Prime Coalition, Elemental Excelerator, ARPA-E.
  - Defense: In-Q-Tel, AFVentures, NSIN.
  - Agriculture: USDA, FFAR, food/ag accelerators.
  - Education: Strada Education, Lumina, ED IES grants.
  - Manufacturing: Manufacturing USA institutes, 15+ across domains.
  - Space: SpaceFund, Seraphim, NASA programs.
  - Fintech: bank partnerships, CDFI funds.

### 5. Debt & Revenue-Based Financing

- Venture debt: Silicon Valley Bank successors, Hercules, TriplePoint.
- Revenue-based financing: Capchase, Pipe, Lighter Capital, Clearco.
- SaaS financing: Founderpath, Arc.
- SBA loans: 7(a), 504, microloans.
- CDFI loans, equipment financing, invoice factoring.
- Lines of credit: Brex, Mercury, Arc.
- **Specialty Lending:** merchant cash advances; recurring revenue loans from Pipe, Capchase, Founderpath; crypto-collateralized loans; securities-based lending; life insurance loans against whole life policies; 401(k) business financing (ROBS).
- **Asset-Based Financing:** equipment leasing; inventory financing; accounts receivable factoring; purchase order financing; warehouse receipts for commodities; IP-backed loans; real estate financing for asset-heavy businesses.

### 6. Customer & Revenue-Based Capital

- Pre-orders and deposits: the Tesla model, with $1K-$10K deposits funding production before delivery.
- Letters of intent (LOIs) that can become financing through Pipe or Capchase.
- Annual prepay discounts: for example, 20% off annual contracts for upfront cash.
- Customer-funded development where enterprise customers pay for desired features.
- Design partnerships where early enterprise customers pay $50K-$500K to shape the product.
- Milestone payments from large government or enterprise contracts.

### 7. Government Contracts (Not Grants)

- GSA Schedules / Multiple Award Schedules: pre-approved vendor lists.
- SAM.gov: central federal contracting portal.
- Set-asides: 8(a), HUBZone, WOSB, SDVOSB, Native American 8(a).
- OTAs (Other Transaction Authorities): flexible DoD contracting, faster than traditional procurement.
- CSO (Commercial Solutions Opening): DoD non-traditional contracting.
- State and local procurement: every state, city, school district, and hospital system buys things.
- GovTribe, Bloomberg Government, FedBizOpps as discovery tools.
- Procurement Technical Assistance Centers, now APEX Accelerators: free help for small businesses to win contracts.
- **Government Direct Investment:** In-Q-Tel, AFVentures, NavalX, Army Applications Lab, NSIN, state sovereign-wealth-style funds, CHIPS Act direct funding, and DOE Loan Programs Office with $400B+ available.

### 8. Tax Credits & Incentives

- R&D Tax Credit at federal and state levels.
- Qualified Small Business Stock (QSBS).
- Work Opportunity Tax Credit.
- Investment Tax Credits for energy and other categories.
- Inflation Reduction Act incentives for clean energy.
- CHIPS Act funding for semiconductors.
- **Energy & Climate Incentives:** federal ITC/PTC; IRA credits with direct pay for clean energy; 45Q carbon capture tax credit; state renewable energy credits (RECs / SRECs); carbon credit markets; utility rebates and demand response programs; green banks such as Connecticut Green Bank, NY Green Bank, and Coalition for Green Capital; DOE Loan Programs Office with $400B+ available.

### 9. Non-Cash Resources & Credits

- Cloud credits from AWS Activate, Google for Startups, Azure for Startups, often $100K+.
- Stripe Atlas and software credits.
- University tech transfer offices.
- Incubator memberships, often with free space.
- Federal lab partnerships through CRADAs.
- **Bartering & Trade Credits:** B2B barter exchanges such as BizX and ITEX; equity-for-services deals with vendors, lawyers, and advisors; sweat equity arrangements with co-founders and advisors; cloud credits as quasi-currency.

### 11. Sponsored Research & University Partnerships

- Industry-sponsored research agreements at universities where the company funds research and gets first rights.
- NSF I/UCRC (Industry/University Cooperative Research Centers).
- CRADA (Cooperative R&D Agreements) with federal labs such as NREL, ORNL, Sandia, Argonne.
- NASA Space Act Agreements.
- DOE National Lab partnerships, including the context for Activate Fellowship.
- University tech transfer offices for licensing out of or into academia.
- Endowed chairs and professorships funding research aligned with a company.

### 12. Strategic Partnerships & Joint Ventures

- Licensing deals: license technology to larger players for upfront payments plus royalties.
- OEM / white-label agreements: get paid to embed in another product.
- Co-development agreements where a partner funds R&D in exchange for rights.
- Channel partner advances where distributors pay for exclusive rights.
- Acquisition advances or acqui-hire discussions that can turn into investment rounds.

### 13. Crowdfunding & Community Capital

- **Donation-Based:** GoFundMe; Kickstarter; Indiegogo; Patreon; Substack; Open Collective; GitHub Sponsors; ko-fi; Buy Me a Coffee.
- **Cause-Specific Donation Platforms:** Global Giving, Donors Choose, Charity Navigator, Giving Multiplier, Manifund, Lightspeed Grants.
- **Cooperative & Mutual Capital:** credit unions, CDFIs, MDIs, cooperative business models, ROSCAs / tandas / susus / hui, Kiva 0% interest crowdfunded loans up to $15K, B Lab / B Corp networks.
- **Quasi-Government / Non-Profit Lenders:** SCORE, Small Business Development Centers (SBDCs), Minority Business Development Agency (MBDA), Women's Business Centers, Native American CDFIs, Working Solutions, Accion, Grameen America.

### 14. Sponsorships & Brand Deals

- Conference sponsorships received if the company runs events.
- Content sponsorships for newsletters, podcasts, and YouTube.
- Affiliate revenue.
- Brand partnerships for consumer brands.

### 15. Personal & Founder Capital

- Founder savings and bootstrapping.
- Friends and family rounds, typically $25K-$250K.
- Home equity loans and HELOCs.
- Personal credit cards: Brex, Ramp, Capital One Spark; risky but common.
- 0% APR credit card stacking.
- Personal loans: SoFi, LightStream.
- Cash-out refinancing.
- Selling personal assets.

### 16. Token & Web3 Funding

- Token launches, ICOs, and IDOs.
- Grants from L1s and L2s: Ethereum Foundation, Optimism RetroPGF, Arbitrum, Solana, Polygon, Near.
- Gitcoin Grants and Giveth quadratic funding for public goods.
- DAO treasury grants.

### 17. Real Estate-Related Funding

- TIF (Tax Increment Financing) for development projects.
- EB-5 visa investments where foreign investors receive green cards for $800K+ investment.
- Opportunity Zone investments.
- NMTC (New Markets Tax Credits) for low-income community investment.
- Historic Tax Credits for renovating historic buildings.
- Brownfield grants for cleaning contaminated sites.

### 18. Trade & Export Financing

- EXIM Bank (Export-Import Bank of the US): loans, guarantees, and insurance for exporters.
- OPIC / DFC (Development Finance Corporation): emerging market investments.
- SBA Export Express and Export Working Capital.
- State export promotion grants.
- Trade credit insurance.

### 19. Agricultural-Specific Funding

- USDA loans through FSA (Farm Service Agency).
- Farm Credit System: cooperatively owned lender with $400B+ in assets.
- Conservation Reserve Program payments.
- Crop insurance and commodity payments.
- Value-Added Producer Grants.

### 20. Carbon, Biodiversity & Environmental Markets

- Carbon offset sales through verified credits such as Verra, Gold Standard, and Puro.
- Biodiversity credits, an emerging market.
- Conservation easements.
- Wetland mitigation banks.
- Water rights trading.

### 21. Insurance & Risk-Based Capital

- Key person insurance payouts.
- Business interruption insurance.
- R&D insurance products, an emerging category.
- Warranty and extended service revenue.

### 22. Data, IP & Asset Monetization

- Selling anonymized data.
- Patent sales or licensing.
- Domain name sales.
- Trademark licensing.
- Excess capacity monetization: unused compute, storage, bandwidth, or office space.

### 23. "Reverse" Funding Mechanisms

- Acquisitions of revenue: buying small profitable businesses to fund a moonshot through HoldCo or search fund models.
- Roll-up strategies with private equity backing.
- SPAC mergers.
- Direct listings.

### 24. Time-Specific Emergency Funding

- Disaster relief through SBA disaster loans and FEMA grants.
- COVID-era programs such as PPP and EIDL, mostly closed but illustrative of recurring crisis-response patterns.
- Black swan event response funds that periodically emerge.

### 25. Insurance Settlements & Legal Wins

- Patent litigation settlements, including Intellectual Ventures-style monetization.
- Class action settlements.
- Insurance claims that fund a pivot or restart.

### 26. Lottery & Gambling-Adjacent

- State lottery-funded programs, since many state grant programs are funded by lottery revenue.
- Prize-linked savings accounts.
- Innovation lotteries in some countries.

### 27. Religious & Community Funding

- Catholic Charities and Jewish Federations, which often have business loan or grant programs.
- Islamic finance / Qard al-Hasan interest-free loans.
- Church-affiliated CDFIs.
- Tribal nations with economic development grants for Native-owned businesses.

### 28. Indirect / Adjacent Capital

- Lower CAC through free distribution such as App Store or Product Hunt features.
- Press and PR such as Forbes 30 Under 30 and Inc. 5000 that can lead to inbound deals and funding.
- Awards with no cash but valuable introductions: Endeavor Entrepreneur, World Economic Forum Technology Pioneer.
- YC Bookface and accelerator networks for warm intros to capital.

## Part II - Individual & Demographic-Based Funding

These sources fund individuals such as founders, students, and researchers rather than companies directly. They are less relevant for a startup as an entity, but highly relevant for founders personally; the same demographic matching logic often unlocks founder-side capital.

## Notes on Using This List

- **Stage and structure determine eligibility.** A pre-revenue solo founder qualifies for a different set of sources than a Series A team of 40. A YC-backed Delaware C-corp may not apply directly for most foundation grants, but it can win prize challenges or receive Program-Related Investments.
- **Stack non-dilutive sources before going equity.** SBIR, R&D tax credits, cloud credits, and pitch competition wins can add up to $1M+ before giving up a share.
- **Geographic and local sources are the biggest blind spot.** Detroit Economic Development Corp grants, Wayne County minority business funds, Michigan Strategic Fund programs, and similar local sources change constantly and require local intelligence.
- **Demographic eligibility unlocks parallel funding pools.** Set-asides such as 8(a), WOSB, HUBZone, and SDVOSB, plus demographic-focused VCs such as Backstage, Visible Hands, Unshackled, and Cartier, are underutilized because qualifying founders often do not know they qualify.
- **Customer-funded development is underrated.** Pre-orders, LOIs, design partnerships, and milestone payments fund growth without dilution or debt service while validating product-market fit.
