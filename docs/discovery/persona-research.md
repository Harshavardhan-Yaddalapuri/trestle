## 2. Why Maurinne Dismissed $100K Grants

Medtech founders operate on drastically longer timelines than SaaS founders:
- **Class II medical device:** $2M–$15M to market.
- **Class III medical device:** $15M–$100M+ to market.
- **Timeline:** Preclinical → regulatory submission → clinical trials → FDA/CE clearance → commercial — often **5–10 years** before first revenue.
- **SBIR Phase I grants:** Typically $300K–$500K. They fund feasibility studies, not productization.
- **SBIR Phase II:** Up to $2M–$3M. But these are competitive (15–20% approval rate) and take 6–24 months to award.
- **Real cost of a clinical trial:** $500K–$5M+ depending on device class and endpoints.

A $100K grant covers maybe 2–3 months of burn for a team of 2–3 scientists. It is effectively a rounding error. It does not move the regulatory needle. It does not pay for a CRO. It does not cover FDA submission costs. Founders like Maurinne's clients **know this**. A tool that surfaces $100K grants as "funding opportunities" loses credibility instantly.

**What they actually need:**
1. **Phase II+ non-dilutive grants** (SBIR/STTR, DoD, ARPA-H, BARDA, NSF — $1M–$3M range).
2. **Strategic grant sequencing**: Which grants to stack and in what order to minimize equity dilution.
3. **Regulatory milestone funding**: Grants that specifically cover clinical trial costs, CRO fees, FDA submission prep.
4. **Venture bridging**: When to transition from grants to angels/VCs, and how much runway that buys.

## 4. What Makes MedTech Founders Different from SaaS Founders

| Dimension | SaaS Founder | MedTech Founder |
|-----------|------------|----------------|
| **Time to Revenue** | 6–18 months | 3–10 years |
| **Capital Need** | $500K–$5M to product/market fit | $2M–$100M+ to first sale |
| **Regulatory Barrier** | Minimal (GDPR/privacy) | FDA / CE / MDR / ISO 13485 — can be years |
| **Key Milestones** | MRR, user growth, churn | Preclinical data, clinical evidence, regulatory clearance |
| **Investor Traction** | Revenue + metrics | Regulatory progress + clinical publications + KOL support |
| **Burn Rate Relation** | Revenue scales with users | Burn is fixed until clearance, then step-change |
| **Grant Relevance** | Low (mostly equity/revenue-based) | **High** (grants are primary non-dilutive source) |
| **IP Strategy** | Trade secrets, patents optional | Patents critical; IP thicket common |
| **Team Skills** | Engineering, design, sales | Science, regulatory, clinical, reimbursement |
| **Failure Mode** | Pivot product, change pricing | IP invalidated, trial fails, FDA rejection = death |

**Implication for Trestle:** A conversational agent for medtech founders must know the above viscerally. It cannot default to SaaS playbooks (e.g., "build an MVP, get 10 users, raise a pre-seed". It must surface **funding that matches the regulatory timeline**, not the revenue timeline.

## 5. How Would a Conversational Agent Best Serve Them? (vs. a Form)

Maurinne's existing tool has **80+ questions and still sounds like ChatGPT**. Here's why a form fails for this audience:

### Why the Form Fails
1. ** Static**: Every founder at a different stage needs different questions. A form cannot adapt.
2. ** Credential-blind**: It cannot verify if "post-seed" means "have FDA clearance" or "have a pitch deck."
3. ** Generic output**: It surfaces opportunities without context. "$100K SBIR Phase I" is not relevant to someone who needs $2M for a clinical trial.
4. ** No follow-up**: A founder's situation changes weekly. A form is a snapshot. Funding is fluid.
5. ** Credential-gap**: The founder has to bridge the gap between what the form asks and what the funder needs. The form just outputs a list.

### How a Conversational Agent Succeeds
| Capability | How it serves medtech founders |
|------------|-------------------------------|
| **Adaptive questioning** | Asks 3–5 questions, then pivots. "You said 'preclinical stage' — do you have GLP data or just benchtop?" |
| **Contextual matching** | Does not surface Phase I grants to someone who needs Phase II+ funds. Filters by **funding size**, **stage match**, **regulatory fit**. |
| **Goal-oriented dialogue** | Not "what grants are available?" but "I need $2M to run a 30-patient feasibility study in Q2 2027. What covers that?" |
| **Credibility signals** | Cites precedent: "The last 3 similar companies got funded through ARPA-H. Here is their PI." |
| **Runway modeling** | Calculates: "If you get this $500K Phase I in 6 months, that buys you 10 months. You also need..." |
| **Follow-up memory** | Remembers "last time you said you were submitting to NIH in October" and checks in. |
| **Actionable outputs** | Not a list. A **funding stack**: "Apply to X (request LOA by date), then Y (deadline in 90 days), also speak to Z (warm intro available)." |
| **Regulatory fluency** | Asks device class, regulatory pathway, clinical data status, then filters opportunities accordingly. |

### What the Agent Should NOT Do
- Do NOT sound like a generic ChatGPT. No "As a language model..." No "I'm here to help you explore options."
- Do NOT ask 80 questions. 3–5 adaptive questions max. The rest is inferred or asked contextually.
- Do NOT output lists of 50 grants. Filter to 3–5 **highly relevant** ones with **why this fits**.
- Do NOT ignore the **size filter**. If the founder says "$500K+", do not surface sub-$300K grants.
- Do NOT treat all founders as SaaS. Language, timelines, milestones, and investor expectations are different.

| EU Horizon / EIC | €2M–€5M | European founders, consortium-ready |

| Source | Size | Best For |
|--------|------|----------|

## 7. Implications for Trestle (Actionable)

1. **Credential check on startup**: The agent must verify — in 1–2 questions — whether the founder has regulatory experience, clinical data, and what their burn rate is. This determines everything that follows.
2. **Grant sizing is a hard filter**: Never surface sub-$300K grants unless the founder explicitly asks for early-stage non-dilutive.
3. **Regulatory stage mapping**: The agent needs an internal model of: Idea → Prototype → Preclinical → IND/IDE → Clinical → FDA/CE → Commercial. Each stage maps to different funders.
4. **Geographic awareness**: US (NIH/NSF/DoD), EU (Horizon/Innovate UK), UK (similar but post-Brexit nuances), APAC (varies wildly). The agent should know which applies.
5. **Conversational memory**: These are **long sales cycles**. A founder engaged in Q2 2026 needs follow-up in Q4 2026 when a new solicitation opens. The agent must remember and nudge.
6. **Investor matching, not just grant matching**: Medtech founders need investors who understand regulated timelines. The agent should tag investors by: "understands FDA process" / "has medtech portfolio" / "writes $X checks" / "lead vs follow."
7. **Integration into Maurinne's workflow**: She runs Nucleate (24 countries). Trestle should be able to ingest cohort data, batch-match founders to opportunities, and let her curate recommendations — not just 1:1 founder use.
