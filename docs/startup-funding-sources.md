# Startup Funding Sources

A comprehensive guide to capital sources, ranked by relevance to startups.

---

## Part I — Startup & Company Funding

### 1. Equity Funding

#### Pre-seed / Seed

- Angel investors and angel groups (AngelList, Golden Seeds, Pipeline Angels)
- Pre-seed funds (Hustle Fund, Precursor, Afore)
- Accelerators (YC, Techstars, 500, MassChallenge, Plug and Play) — typically $125K–$500K for equity
- Studios (Atomic, Pioneer Square Labs, Betaworks)

#### Venture Capital

- Traditional VCs by stage (seed, Series A through late)
- Corporate VCs (GV, Intel Capital, Salesforce Ventures, M12, Comcast Ventures)
- Strategic investors

#### Alternative Equity

- Crowdfunding equity (Republic, Wefunder, StartEngine — Reg CF up to $5M)
- Reg A+ ($75M cap)
- Family offices
- Sovereign wealth funds

#### Family Offices & UHNW Individuals

- Single-family offices (SFOs) — direct deals from wealthy families
- Multi-family offices (MFOs) — Bessemer Trust, BBR Partners, etc.
- Tiger 21, YPO networks
- Angel syndicate platforms (AngelList, Sweater Ventures, Republic)
- Rolling funds
- Searchfund model — for acquiring rather than starting

### 3. Non-Dilutive Funding (Grants & Awards)

#### Federal Government (US)

- **SBIR/STTR** — Small Business Innovation Research and Small Business Technology Transfer programs across 11 participating agencies (NIH, NSF, DOE, DoD, NASA, USDA, DOT, ED, EPA, DHS, DOC). ~$4B/year. Phase I ($50K–$300K), Phase II ($750K–$2M), Phase III (commercialization).
- **NSF** — beyond SBIR: Convergence Accelerator, Partnerships for Innovation, I-Corps
- **NIH** — Direct grants (R01, R21, R43/R44), specific institute funding
- **DOE** — ARPA-E, loan guarantees, Office of Technology Transitions
- **DoD** — DIU, AFWERX, SOFWERX, Army xTechSearch, Navy SBIR
- **NASA** — iTech, Tipping Point
- **USDA** — Rural Business Development Grants, REAP
- **EPA** — Green Chemistry, environmental tech grants
- **NIST** — Manufacturing extension, advanced manufacturing
- **DARPA** — Solicitations for breakthrough tech
- **IARPA** — Intelligence community R&D
- **BARDA** — Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority (biodefense, pandemic preparedness)

#### State & Local

- State economic development agencies (every state has one)
- State-specific SBIR matching programs
- City-level innovation funds (NYCEDC, LA, Boston, etc.)
- Opportunity Zones programs
- State tax credits (R&D, hiring, investment)
- State Small Business Credit Initiative (SSBCI) programs — every state now has one

#### International Grants

- **EU** — Horizon Europe, EIC Accelerator (up to €17.5M), Eureka
- **UK** — Innovate UK, British Business Bank
- **Canada** — IRAP, SR&ED tax credits, Strategic Innovation Fund

- **Singapore** — Enterprise Singapore, Startup SG

- **Australia** — R&D Tax Incentive, Accelerating Commercialisation
- **Japan** — J-Startup, NEDO

#### Industry-Specific Grant Programs

- **Biotech/Health** — BARDA, CARB-X, JDRF T1D Fund, disease foundations
- **Climate** — Breakthrough Energy, Prime Coalition, Elemental Excelerator, ARPA-E
- **Defense** — In-Q-Tel, AFVentures, NSIN

### 5. Debt & Revenue-Based Financing

- Venture debt (Silicon Valley Bank successors, Hercules, TriplePoint)
- Revenue-based financing (Capchase, Pipe, Lighter Capital, Clearco)
- SaaS financing (Founderpath, Arc)
- SBA loans (7(a), 504, microloans)
- CDFI loans
- Equipment financing
- Invoice factoring
- Lines of credit (Brex, Mercury, Arc)

#### Specialty Lending

- Merchant cash advances (expensive, but fast)
- Recurring revenue loans — Pipe, Capchase, Founderpath
- Crypto-collateralized loans — borrow against crypto holdings
- Securities-based lending — borrow against your own stock portfolio
- Life insurance loans — borrow against whole life policies
- 401(k) business financing (ROBS) — use retirement funds to start a business without penalty

#### Asset-Based Financing

- Equipment leasing — preserve cash by leasing instead of buying
- Inventory financing — borrow against unsold inventory
- Accounts receivable factoring — sell invoices for immediate cash
- Purchase order financing — get capital to fulfill a confirmed PO
- Warehouse receipts (commodities)
- IP-backed loans — borrow against patent portfolios
- Real estate financing for asset-heavy businesses

#### Fiscal Sponsorship — A Key Mechanism

Most founders don't know this exists. If you're a startup with charitable mission components, a fiscal sponsor (Tides, Players Philanthropy Fund, Social Good Fund, etc.) lets you receive tax-deductible donations without forming your own 501(c)(3). This unlocks the entire donation ecosystem for hybrid for-profit/nonprofit structures.

## Notes on Using This List

1. **Stage and structure determine eligibility.** A pre-revenue solo founder qualifies for a totally different set than a Series A team of 40. A YC-backed Delaware C-corp can't apply for most foundation grants directly, but they can win a prize challenge or receive a Program-Related Investment (PRI).

2. **Stack non-dilutive sources before going equity.** SBIR + R&D tax credits + cloud credits + a pitch competition win can add up to $1M+ before giving up a single share.

3. **Geographic / local sources are the biggest blind spot.** National databases cover federal grants and major VCs well. The Detroit Economic Development Corp grant, the Wayne County minority business fund, the Michigan Strategic Fund — these change constantly and require local intelligence.

4. **Demographic eligibility unlocks parallel funding pools.** Set-asides (8(a), WOSB, HUBZone, SDVOSB) and demographic-focused VCs (Backstage, Visible Hands, Unshackled, Cartier) are some of the most underutilized sources because qualifying founders often don't know they qualify.

5. **Customer-funded development is the most underrated source.** Pre-orders, LOIs, design partnerships, and milestone payments fund growth without dilution or debt service — and validate product-market fit at the same time.
